MARS LANDER — ESP32 WIRING STRUCTURE
Version: GPIO Map V1 (proposed)
Board: ESP32 DevKit V1, ESP32-WROOM-32
Purpose: Keep lander GPIO assignments consistent across firmware, wiring, and documentation.

IMPORTANT STATUS
- This is the agreed proposed pin map, not a verified physical wiring diagram.
- Verify the exact ESP32 board variant and each module's electrical requirements before powering anything.
- Do not change GPIO assignments without updating this file and the pin definitions at the top of lander.ino.
- GPIO 21/22 are shared by the LCD and BMP280 on one I2C bus; verify their I2C addresses do not conflict.

2. SHARED I2C BUS
- SDA: GPIO 21
- SCL: GPIO 22
- Connect LCD I2C backpack and BMP280 SDA/SCL to the same bus.
- Verify their I2C addresses are different.
- Check module logic-voltage compatibility; do not assume every I2C backpack is safe at 3.3V logic.

3. POWER AND GROUND RULES
- Do NOT power MG996R servos from the ESP32 3.3V pin.
- Power both servos from a suitably rated regulated external supply/BEC.
- Do not route servo current through ordinary breadboard power rails.
- The WS2812B strip needs a suitable 5V supply sized for the actual strip and brightness.
- Connect grounds together where required: ESP32, servo supply, and LED-strip supply need a common reference for control signals.
- Verify the WS2812B data input accepts the ESP32's 3.3V signal; use a suitable level shifter if required.
- ESP32 GPIOs use 3.3V logic and are not 5V tolerant. Never connect unknown-voltage signals to GPIOs.
- Use suitable series resistors for the green and red indicator LEDs.
- Avoid powering high-current loads from the ESP32 board regulator.

4. INPUT WIRING (PROPOSED)
- Physical button: proposed INPUT_PULLUP configuration; connect switch between GPIO 23 and GND. Pressed reads LOW; released reads HIGH.
- Limit-switch NO/NC contacts must be selected based on the actual switches and final safety design.
- Do not assume limit-switch HIGH/LOW meaning until wiring is confirmed and documented.
- Debounce the physical button and limit switches as appropriate.

5. FIRMWARE BEHAVIOUR DEPENDENCIES
- One physical button toggles the gate only when the gate is confirmed OPEN or CLOSED.
- Dashboard uses explicit OPEN_GATE and CLOSE_GATE commands.
- Reject all new gate movement commands while gate is OPENING or CLOSING; do not queue commands.
- At startup, read both limit switches:
  * Closed switch active only -> CLOSED
  * Open switch active only -> OPEN
  * Neither active -> UNKNOWN
  * Both active -> FAULT
- Do not automatically move the gate at startup.
- If movement times out without endpoint confirmation, enter FAULT and reject further gate movements until handled.
- STANDBY: LCD displays STANDBY MODE; WS2812B strip remains illuminated; physical gate button, gate movement, and sensor polling are disabled. USB serial remains available for SET_ACTIVE and GET_STATUS.
- If STANDBY is requested during gate movement, finish movement and confirm endpoint before entering STANDBY.
- ACTIVE LCD rotation is approximately every 6 seconds:
  1. Lander/gate status
  2. BMP280 temperature and pressure
  3. DHT11 temperature and humidity
  4. Rover/drone status and USB link status
- Dashboard displays both temperatures, pressure, humidity, gate state, and switch states simultaneously.
- WS2812B uses a slow, colourful space-themed animation and remains on in ACTIVE and STANDBY.

6. BOARD CAUTIONS
- GPIOs 6-11 are typically connected to SPI flash on classic ESP32-WROOM-32 boards; do not use them for peripherals.
- GPIOs 34-39 are input-only; do not assign outputs to them.
- Avoid boot-strapping pins unless their startup behaviour has been checked.
- This map assumes the classic ESP32-WROOM-32 DevKit V1. Recheck if the board variant differs.

7. CHANGE CONTROL
If any GPIO changes:
1. Update this file.
2. Update pin constants at the top of lander.ino.
3. Update physical wiring and component labels.
4. Update team documentation.
5. Re-test startup, gate movement, limit switches, sensors, LEDs, and serial communication.
